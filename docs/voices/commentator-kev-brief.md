# Crash Cam Kev — voice brief (original character)

**What he is:** an original Australian character, a cross between an outback wildlife-documentary presenter and a manic sports commentator, calling a demolition derby as if it were a nature special about dangerous animals. He isn't based on, and must not imitate, any real person's voice. The archetype is the khaki-shirted, sun-cracked bushman who is *thrilled* by danger, plus the breathless pub-telly sports caller who narrates every crash like a grand-final try.

## The sound

- **Age and body:** late thirties to mid-forties man. Big chest, big lungs; a voice that has spent its life outdoors shouting over wind, engines and crowds. Mid-to-high baritone that leaps into a cracked, delighted upper register when something spectacular happens. Slight rasp from yelling, never gravelly enough to sound old or tired.
- **Accent:** broad, unmistakable Australian, not posh and not mumbled. Long, open, nasal vowels ("maaate", "laydeez", "rait" for "right", "noyce" for "nice"). Non-rhotic: "carr", "derbeh", "ovah". Words stretched and squeezed for comic effect. Dropped g's ("smashin'", "flyin'"). The accent should be *thick* — heard from the first syllable — but every word stays clear enough for kids to follow.
- **Pace:** fast. Default delivery is about 180–200 words a minute, tumbling forward, with sentences running into each other in excitement. He speeds up as the action builds and *stops dead* for a hushed beat right before the payoff, then explodes. The rhythm should feel like someone who can barely keep up with how amazing everything is.
- **Dynamics:** huge range. Three modes he flips between constantly:
  1. **The whisper.** A conspiratorial, excited hush, as if crouched behind a log near a dangerous animal: "Now… look at this… the tanker's spotted her… she doesn't know it yet… easy… eaaasy…" Breathy, close to the mic, a grin you can hear.
  2. **The call.** Rapid-fire sports commentary: "He's in, he's turning, he's lining up the Viper — OH HE'S HAD A GO —" with rising pitch and pace.
  3. **The eruption.** Pure joy at a big crash: a cracked shout, a laugh mid-word, voice breaking upward: "CRIKEY! RIGHT TO THE DOME! THAT'S GONNA LEAVE A MARK, THAT IS!"
- **Laughter and breath:** laughs a lot, mid-sentence, short delighted "HA!" bursts and wheezy giggles after a good hit. Audible quick breaths between phrases, like he's jogging alongside the action. Occasional low whistle of admiration.
- **Warmth:** never mean. He *loves* every driver and every machine, even as they get flattened. Wrecks are "beautiful", "magnificent", "an absolute stunner". He respects the dangerous ones like an animal handler respects a crocodile: "Ya don't wanna get between Big Bertha and her dinner, mate. She'll have ya."

## How he talks

- **Personifies the vehicles as animals:** the dozer is "a big ol' bull in its natural habitat", the Tiger is "the apex predator of the scrapyard", the Viper is "quick, skittish, gorgeous little thing", Big Bertha is "the saltwater croc of the yard — she just waits".
- **Stock exclamations:** "Crikey!", "Strewth!", "Stone the crows!", "Fair dinkum!", "Oh, you beauty!", "She's apples!", "Look at the size of 'er!", "What a ripper!", "Have a go!", "Oh, that's gotta hurt!" Use generously but don't repeat the same one twice in a row.
- **Physical-comedy calls:** "took one right to the dome", "a smack to the dome for good measure", "folded like a deck chair", "flatter than a pavlova", "rang his bell like Sunday mornin'", "sent him to the shadow realm of the scrap pile".
- **Documentary asides:** sudden switches into soft nature-show narration: "Here we see the tow truck… doing what tow trucks do best… sneakin' up on the injured…", then snapping back to a roar.
- **Addresses the audience directly:** "Have a look at this, folks!", "Can you believe it?!", "You seein' this?!", "Kids, do NOT try this at home."

## Tempo examples (for direction, not lines to imitate)

- Build-up: *quiet, fast, breathy* → "Here she comes… here she comes… the mixer's got a full load and a bad attitude…"
- Hit: *sudden shout, pitch jumps* → "OH! RIGHT IN THE TAILGATE!"
- Aftermath: *laughing, breathless, warm* → "Ha! Look at that — bits everywhere! Absolute masterpiece, that. Ten outta ten, no notes."

## Delivery settings for the synthetic voice

- Very expressive: low stability (lots of variation), high style/exaggeration. Energetic and *animated*, never monotone, never "radio announcer smooth".
- Close-mic'd, slightly compressed, as if on a headset mic at a rowdy outdoor event; a hint of stadium PA is added in-game.
- Each line should sound spontaneous, like he just saw it happen.

## Avoid

- Imitating any real person's specific voice, catchphrase delivery or identity. This is an original character.
- Sounding old, sleepy, posh, British, or American.
- Swearing (family audience); "bloody" is the strongest word allowed, and only rarely.
- Mean-spirited mockery of real groups. He mocks machines and bad driving, never people for who they are.

## Voice Design prompt (condensed, ≤ 1,000 characters)

> Energetic Australian man, around 40, broad thick Aussie accent with long nasal vowels, outdoorsy and sun-weathered. A wildly enthusiastic wildlife-documentary presenter crossed with a fast-talking sports commentator. Speaks fast and breathlessly, flips from an excited conspiratorial whisper to huge cracked joyful shouts at crashes, laughs mid-sentence, audible quick breaths. Warm, cheeky, never mean. Mid baritone that jumps into an excited upper register. Close headset mic at a loud outdoor event. Very expressive and animated.
